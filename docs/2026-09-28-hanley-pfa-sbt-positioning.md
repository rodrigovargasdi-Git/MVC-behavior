## 2. What PFA, the IISCA and SBT are

**In plain language (for parents)**
> First we find out, safely and quickly, **what a child's difficult behaviour gets for them**. Often it is several things at once: "stop asking me to do this, give me my tablet back, and let me decide". Then we **teach the child better ways to get exactly that**, step by step. They learn to ask, then to cope with "wait" or "not now", and then to join in with more of what the day asks of them, while staying calm and in control.

**Technically**

| Component | What it is | Key design choices |
|---|---|---|
| **PFA: Practical Functional Assessment** | An **open-ended interview** with caregivers, sometimes a brief observation, then the **IISCA** | Uses what caregivers know. Designed to be feasible in clinics, homes and schools. |
| **IISCA: Interview-Informed Synthesized Contingency Analysis** | A type of functional analysis with **one test condition** that combines ("synthesizes") the triggers and consequences caregivers describe, and **one control condition** where all of those good things are freely available | Short sessions. The situation is ended at the **first sign** of problem behaviour (often at mild precursors), so severe episodes are avoided. The aim is a quick, safe, clear result that treatment can be built on. |
| **SBT: Skill-Based Treatment** | Uses the **same synthesized contingency** to teach, in sequence: **communication** (first a simple, then a more complete request), **toleration** (accepting "wait" or "no"), and **cooperation** (contextually appropriate behaviour, with expectations increasing gradually) | Progress only while the learner is **happy, relaxed and engaged**. The learner can always ask for a break. Generalisation to parents, teachers and settings follows (FTF Level 5 content). |

**Honest point for parent pages:** the IISCA **briefly and deliberately recreates situations that usually lead to problem behaviour**, under safe conditions, and ends them straight away. Say this plainly: it builds trust, and hiding it would be misleading.

## 3. "Compassionate care", trauma-informed and assent-based: the framing and the evidence

**The framing ("Today's ABA", coined by Hanley in 2020)**
- **Listen:** learn about preferences, dislikes and communication from the person and from the people who know them.
- **Create joy:** safe, enriched settings where the person leads.
- **Empower:** the person can **say no or walk away** without needing problem behaviour to do so.
- **Teach from engagement:** teach only when the person is **happy, relaxed and engaged (HRE)**.
- **Trauma-informed:** assume that people referred for severe behaviour may have experienced adverse events. Avoid re-traumatising them. Build predictability, trust and control. Do not work people through distress or non-compliance.
- FTF explicitly distances "Today's ABA" from older practices based on dominance, coercion and restraint, and acknowledges harm done in the field's past.
- **Assent-based** means the learner's ongoing willingness, *shown through behaviour*, is a condition for teaching. Withdrawn assent (turning away, protest, a break request) is honoured, not overridden.

**The evidence base (what can honestly be said)**

*Supportive*
- The founding study (Hanley et al., 2014) and many replications report large reductions in problem behaviour and **high caregiver ratings of acceptability**.
- Example: an outpatient series of **25 applications** (Jessel et al., 2018) reported reductions of 90 % or more for every participant, with high social-validity ratings.
- There are replications in homes, schools and other countries (e.g. Strand & Eldevik, 2018), and newer work on performance-based IISCAs (2025/26, PMC).

*Limits and critiques (address them, don't hide them)*
1. **Design:** almost all studies are **single-case experimental designs** (strong internal control, small numbers). There are no large randomised trials, and many studies come from Hanley's group or collaborators.
2. **Scientific debate about the IISCA:** Fisher, Greer and colleagues (2016; Greer et al., 2020), with Holehan et al. and Retzlaff et al. (2020), compared synthesized analyses with standard functional analyses. They reported **possible false positives**: a synthesized test can include consequences that do not actually maintain the behaviour. The Hanley group answers that the test that matters is **treatment utility**, i.e. whether the resulting treatment works and is acceptable. The debate is ongoing (e.g. a 2025 paper in *Perspectives on Behavior Science* on the "isolation vs synthesis" divide).
3. **Germany:** the AWMF autism therapy guideline **expired in March 2026** (Autismus-Stiftung, 29.04.2026). No German guideline was found naming PFA or SBT (**verify**). Under HWG/UWG, "evidenzbasiert", "wissenschaftlich bewiesen" or quoting study percentages as expected outcomes are **risky** (research doc §8). **Safe:** "a research-based approach published in peer-reviewed journals", with 2–3 citations.

## 4. How PFA-SBT answers the common criticism of ABA

| Criticism (e.g. "aba therapie kritik", autistic self-advocates) | How PFA-SBT / Today's ABA responds | What Marija should still say honestly |
|---|---|---|
| **"ABA is compliance training."** | Communication and toleration come *first*. Cooperation is taught only from a calm, engaged state. The learner can always ask for a break or say no. | The target is **self-advocacy and shared routines**, not obedience. Show a concrete example. |
| **"It forces children through distress (escape extinction, physical guidance)."** | FTF's principles reject working people through duress. Assent is monitored continuously. | State her own **commitments**: no physical prompting through refusal, no withholding of breaks, and immediate stop when the child shows distress. |
| **"It trains masking and normalisation."** | The targets are behaviours that harm or limit the person (self-injury, aggression, severe meltdowns), not autistic traits. The person's own "yes/no" is strengthened. | Say explicitly: harmless stimming, eye contact and "looking typical" are **not** goals. Goals are agreed with the family and, where possible, with the child. |
| **"Autistic people are not listened to."** | "Learning by listening": open interviews, and the person's preferences drive the setting. | Add autistic perspectives, e.g. neurodiversity-affirming reading and co-produced content. **Acknowledge that criticism of historic ABA is justified.** |
| **"It is 40 hours a week at a table."** | The model is **caregiver-involved and time-limited**, embedded in daily routines. | Describe dosage honestly (e.g. weekly sessions plus parent practice), with no intensity claims. |
| **"Trauma."** | Explicitly trauma-informed (Rajaraman et al., 2022): predictability, choice, trust, no coercion. | Don't claim "trauma-free" or "healing". Say "trauma-informed practice". |

## 5. FTF: official credentialing structure (as published)

### 5.1 The 7 levels

| Level | Name | What it verifies (paraphrased) | Typical route |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Education** | Can implement PFA-SBT under close, direct supervision | 10-h on-demand course, 12-h training or 2-day live workshop, then an application |
| 2 | **Application** | Can design and implement an assessment and treatment plan with guidance and supervision | Level 1 + workshop or design session with an FTF consultant + assessment workbook. **Can be skipped.** |
| 3 | **Support** | Can design and implement the process under supervision | Serve as the support implementer beside a Level 4 candidate (a "dyad") under a Level 7 consultant; checklist + verification letter |
| **4** | **Lead** | Can **independently design and directly implement** the process with a client, with **intermittent** supervision | Level 4 course *or* a 3-client multiple-exemplar consultation (at least 8 weeks, weekly reviews); checklist + verification letter from a **Level 7** consultant. **Jumping from Level 1 to Level 4 is common** (e.g. the "Introductory Course … Levels 1–4 Credentialing Included"). |
| 5 | **Advanced** | Can design treatment extensions, generalisation and maintenance | Level 4 + advanced coursework, design session, workbook. **Can be folded into Level 4 or 6.** |
| **6** | **Supervision** | Can **independently supervise Level 1–4 practitioners** and support implementation teams | Advanced live course or train-the-trainer consultation: **coach at least 2 new teams** from case conceptualisation to implementation under an FTF instructor (the course lists 37 h of instruction). Needs Level 5, a checklist, a verification letter, 2 documented client cases with video/data evidence and 8 weekly meeting forms. **Requires an independent practitioner credential** (BCBA, SLP, licensed special-ed teacher, LCSW/LMSW). |
| 7 | **Consultation** | Develops implementers and supervisors (details "forthcoming" on the 2026 page) | By FTF |

**Also relevant**
- An **alternative pathway** exists for experienced practitioners (scored submission with a rubric).
- Only FTF training completed **after 1 January 2019** counts.
- Applications take **at least 45 days**.
- The credential comes with a **PDF certificate, a wallet card and a letter signed by Dr. Hanley**.
- Directory listing needs consent (a checkbox).
- Holders get access to an exclusive seminar ("Big Ideas and New Discoveries").
- **FTF accepts applicants regardless of where they live.**
- No recognition by the BACB or any regulator is mentioned. In Germany it is a **private qualification record**, which is valuable as proof of training but has no legal status.

## 6. BCBA vs IBA vs QBA vs RBT: what applies to someone with a US education working in Germany

| Credential | Body | Level | Status for people **living in Germany** (09/2026) | Relevance for Marija |
|---|---|---|---|---|

She must add the **country disclosure**: say that BACB certification is **not currently issued in Germany**. |
| **BCaBA** | BACB | Bachelor's | Same restrictions | – |
| **RBT** | BACB | Technician (works under BCBA supervision) | Same restrictions | Not for her. Relevant for tutors she might supervise, but no new RBTs in Germany. |
| **IBA** (International Behavior Analyst) | IBAO | Master's route or experience route | Designed for practitioners **outside North America**. Degree, supervised hours (about 1,000–1,500), exam, CEUs. Candidate registration about US$299. Recertification includes 24 CEUs and a mentorship year. | An option if she wants a credential **currently issued in Europe**, or to supervise IBA candidates (eligibility **verify**). |
| **QBA** | QABA (US; international) | Master's | International applicants, remote exams, **public registry**. Fieldwork rises to 2,000 h (transition rules from 2026). | Only if she is not a BCBA and wants a behaviour-analysis credential. |

**Also for a US-educated practitioner in Germany**
- **Germany has no licence for behaviour analysts.** "Verhaltensanalytikerin" is not protected, but it must be true (research doc §8).
- Use foreign degrees **in their original form, with the university and country** (e.g. "Master of Arts in …, [University], USA").
- An optional **ZAB Zeugnisbewertung** (statement of comparability, €200) shows German equivalence. It helps with Jugendämter and Träger, which check staff qualifications.
- **BACB fieldwork supervision** is mostly irrelevant for German residents, who cannot apply. Supervising US- or Canada-resident trainees remotely is a separate question (**verify BACB supervisor rules**).
- Supervision demand in Europe goes to **IBA/QBA candidates**, and, if she is Level 6, to PFA-SBT practitioners.

10. How many PFA-SBT cases have you led, and in which settings (home, Kita, school, Träger)? Is any of this work **Der Steg's** (for the side-activity question)?
11. Do you use PFA-SBT **at Der Steg** today? If yes, the website must not present Der Steg cases, materials or outcomes.
12. Which **commitments** will you state publicly (no physical guidance through refusal, breaks always honoured, no targeting of stimming or eye contact)?
13. Would you offer **PFA-SBT supervision or workshops to professionals** (needs Level 6 for supervision), and in which languages?
